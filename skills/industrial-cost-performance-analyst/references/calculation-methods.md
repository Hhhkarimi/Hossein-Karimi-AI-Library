# Calculation Methods

## Core cost metrics

### Total manufacturing cost
Total Manufacturing Cost = sum of all included manufacturing costs after valid allocations.

### Unit production cost
Unit Cost = Total Manufacturing Cost / Production Quantity

The denominator must match the agreed business definition, e.g. good kg, gross kg, equivalent units, or physical units.

### Cost center share
Cost Center Share % = Cost Center Cost / Total Manufacturing Cost × 100

### Cost center cost per unit
Cost Center Unit Contribution = Cost Center Cost / Production Quantity

## Budget variance
Absolute Variance = Actual - Budget

Variance % = (Actual - Budget) / Budget × 100

When Budget = 0, percentage variance is N/M.

## Prior-period variance
Absolute Change = Current - Prior

Change % = (Current - Prior) / Prior × 100

When Prior = 0, percentage change is N/M.

## Simple volume/rate decomposition
When a cost can be modeled as rate × activity:

Budget Cost = Budget Rate × Budget Activity
Actual Cost = Actual Rate × Actual Activity

Rate Effect = (Actual Rate - Budget Rate) × Actual Activity
Volume/Usage Effect = (Actual Activity - Budget Activity) × Budget Rate

Alternative company conventions may use different anchors. Follow documented policy and state the formula.

## Material variance
Price Variance = (Actual Price - Standard Price) × Actual Quantity
Usage Variance = (Actual Quantity - Standard Quantity for Actual Output) × Standard Price

## Labor variance
Rate Variance = (Actual Rate - Standard Rate) × Actual Hours
Efficiency Variance = (Actual Hours - Standard Hours for Actual Output) × Standard Rate

## Fixed-overhead absorption insight
When fixed manufacturing cost is approximately stable:
Fixed OH per Unit = Fixed Manufacturing OH / Production Volume

A fall in production volume can raise unit cost even without higher total fixed cost. Quantify the denominator effect before describing operational deterioration.
