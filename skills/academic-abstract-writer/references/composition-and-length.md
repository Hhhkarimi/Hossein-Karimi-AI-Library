# Composition And Length

## Compose one coherent abstract

Unless explicitly requested otherwise, write exactly one uninterrupted paragraph with no internal headings, bullets, numbering, line breaks, or separate sections. A localized heading above the paragraph and the keyword line below it do not count as additional abstract paragraphs.

Build the paragraph through the following rhetorical functions, combining or reordering them when natural for the article type:

1. **Context and motivation:** Establish the specific problem or documented gap in one or two concise sentences. Avoid broad opening clichés and unsupported assertions that no prior research exists.
2. **Purpose:** State the precise research objective, question, or central argument. Ensure it follows logically from the problem.
3. **Approach:** Report essential methods or the conceptual approach with enough detail to interpret the evidence. Include relevant design, sample/data, analytical technique, and comparison conditions where supported.
4. **Principal results or arguments:** Give the actual answer to the research question. Prioritize main findings and informative evidence over methodological trivia. For quantitative studies, report the most relevant estimates, directions, units, and uncertainty when available and appropriate. For qualitative or theoretical work, articulate the actual themes, mechanisms, propositions, or arguments instead of forcing numerical results.
5. **Conclusion and contribution:** Explain what the findings establish or suggest, identify the supported contribution, and include a material limitation when it affects interpretation. End with a substantive scientific statement rather than a generic promise of impact.

Keep context concise and allocate the greatest appropriate space to substantive results or arguments. Use a flexible allocation rather than a fixed sentence count or percentage. The paragraph must have a clear logical progression from problem to purpose, approach, evidence, and interpretation. Do not use formulaic labels such as 'Background:' or 'Methods:' inside it unless explicitly requested.

## Handle length precisely

- When the user specifies a maximum, never exceed it. When the user specifies an exact number, target that number and verify it with an available counting tool; otherwise count as carefully as possible and do not claim exact verification without it.
- If a requested range is supplied, remain within it where feasible. Do not turn an exact target into an approximate one without disclosure.
- Use the journal's counting rules if supplied. Otherwise, for space-delimited languages count whitespace-separated tokens, treating Persian half-space compounds as one token. For languages without reliable whitespace boundaries, use the appropriate language-specific segmentation where available. Word processors and publishers may count differently.
- Count the abstract body only by default. Do not count its heading, keywords, or editorial notes.
- Shorten by removing generic context, redundant transitions, and secondary details first. Expand only with meaningful, source-supported information. Preserve the primary result, interpretation, and essential qualifiers.
