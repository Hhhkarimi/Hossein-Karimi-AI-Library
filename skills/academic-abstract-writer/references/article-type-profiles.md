# Article Type Profiles

## Adapt to the article type

Use the following as internal content priorities, not visible section labels. Do not force an empirical template onto a theoretical or interpretive article.

| Article type | Prioritize in the abstract |
| --- | --- |
| Quantitative empirical study | Objective, design, sample/data, analysis, principal quantitative results, uncertainty, bounded interpretation |
| Qualitative study | Phenomenon, context, participants or materials, qualitative approach, central themes or interpretations, contribution |
| Mixed-methods study | Purpose, quantitative and qualitative components, integration approach, integrated findings, interpretation |
| Randomized trial | Design, groups/intervention/comparator, relevant sample counts, primary outcome, effect estimates and uncertainty, important harms or limitations, registration if required |
| Observational study | Design, population, exposure/outcome, key associations, adjustment where relevant, uncertainty, noncausal interpretation unless justified |
| Systematic or scoping review | Review question, eligibility, sources and search date, selection/synthesis approach, included evidence, key findings and limitations |
| Meta-analysis | Included studies, synthesis model where important, pooled effect estimates, precision, relevant heterogeneity and limitations |
| Narrative or critical review | Scope, organizing approach, principal synthesis or critique, knowledge gaps and implications |
| Theoretical, humanities, or conceptual article | Problem, thesis, conceptual or interpretive approach, principal argument or formal result, original contribution |
| Engineering, computing, or methodological paper | Technical problem, proposed approach, validation design, baselines/metrics, key performance results under stated conditions, contribution |
| Case study or case report | Context and case characteristics, approach or intervention, key observations, bounded lessons |
| Replication or validation study | Target finding or instrument, replication/validation approach, principal results, consistency or discrepancy with original work |
| Protocol or registered report before results | Rationale, objective, planned design and analysis, intended contribution; clearly indicate study stage and do not invent findings |

Apply relevant reporting guidance, such as PRISMA, CONSORT, STROBE, STARD, SRQR, APA JARS, or ICMJE, when appropriate to the design and target venue. Do not assert compliance with an entire standard unless its applicable requirements have actually been checked. If exact requirements are unknown and cannot be verified, do not invent them.
