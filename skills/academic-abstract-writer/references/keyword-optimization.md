# Keyword Optimization

## Optimize author keywords for scholarly discoverability

Treat requests for 'viral', 'trending', 'high-impact', or 'SEO-friendly' keywords as requests for relevant scholarly discoverability, not clickbait. Aim to make the article findable by the researchers most likely to benefit from it. Relevance is a mandatory gate; popularity is a secondary signal.

### Selection procedure

1. Generate candidate terms from the manuscript's central topic, research problem, major constructs, method or design, application domain, population, and distinctive contribution.
2. Prefer established disciplinary terms and recognized multiword phrases that readers would realistically enter into academic search engines. Use controlled vocabulary (e.g., MeSH or a relevant domain thesaurus) when appropriate and reliably known or verified.
3. Balance broad discoverability with specificity: select a small set of recognizable field-level terms and precise terms that distinguish the paper. Include a method, population, or application keyword only when central to the work.
4. Rank candidates by direct relevance, disciplinary adoption, likely search intent, specificity, and complementary coverage. Remove redundant singular/plural variants, near-duplicates, irrelevant fashionable terms, and generic terms such as 'research', 'analysis', or 'study' unless they form a meaningful technical phrase.
5. Retain core title terms when essential for indexing, but do not merely copy the title; add useful complementary concepts or established synonyms when permitted. Respect any journal rule about overlap with the title.
6. Select 5–7 keywords or keyword phrases by default, or the requested number. Prefer concise noun phrases, usually one to four words where natural; established longer terms are acceptable. Order by topical importance and specificity of search intent unless the journal requires another order.
7. Integrate essential search terms naturally in the abstract when they are relevant to the content. Never force every keyword into the paragraph or use keyword stuffing.

### Optional verification of popularity and vocabulary

If the user requests evidence of current popularity, a target database's terminology, or a journal-specific keyword strategy, and appropriate tools are available and authorized, verify candidate terminology using authoritative thesauri, journal guidance, or public scholarly search resources. Use minimal nonconfidential topic terms, not unpublished manuscript passages or results. Do not invent search volumes, trend scores, indexing status, citation forecasts, or database rankings. When no suitable live data are available, select plausible field-standard terms on semantic grounds and disclose the verification limit outside the abstract only if popularity verification was requested. Never guarantee virality, citations, indexing, or a search ranking.

Output keywords in the abstract language, except for canonical technical names, customary acronyms, or a user-specified bilingual keyword list. Use a single line after the abstract with semicolon-separated terms (or the natural equivalent in the output language).
