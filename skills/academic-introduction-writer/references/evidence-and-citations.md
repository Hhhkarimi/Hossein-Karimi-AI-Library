# Evidence and citations

## Retrieval

Inspect supplied sources, then retrieve what is needed. Use original studies for specific findings, reviews for overviews, foundational work for origin and official sources for current rules. Include material contrary/null findings. Recency does not replace relevance.

Respect the author's corpus, date, language and search restrictions. In supplied-only mode do not discover external sources; describe the coverage limit. Do not impose an arbitrary minimum source count or fill a quota with weak sources. Ordinary source retrieval does not authorize paid jobs or uploads of unpublished material to another service.

Search synonyms and combinations of construct, population, context and method. Follow citations from nearest work when available. End a targeted search when central claims/comparisons are covered or retrieval is blocked; record why. This is not systematic coverage.

Match title, authors, year, venue and identifier. DOI resolution alone does not verify claim support. Resolve metadata conflicts against publisher/source records. Check corrections/retractions when feasible; disclose if not checked. Abstract-only access supports only abstract-level claims. Snippets are discovery aids.

## Evidence ledger

Separate three dimensions:

- Access: full text, abstract, metadata or none.
- Identity: checked or unchecked.
- Support: supports, contradicts, partial, unresolved or not applicable.

Identity-checked metadata may still have unresolved support. An uploaded text may support a claim while metadata remains unchecked. Author-supplied does not mean verified. Record exact locator when available, limitations and provenance.

For contribution positioning compare question, design, data/population, context, analysis and contribution with closest work. Seek disconfirming evidence. Separate the author's assertion from checked position. Scope claims to actual coverage; a hedge does not rescue an inadequate search.

Never invent authors, titles, dates, DOI, statistics, quotations or findings. Omit, qualify or mark unsupported statements `[SOURCE NEEDED: specific claim]`. Unresolved records belong in notes, not a final bibliography. Author claims and proposals need distinct labels.

## Rendering

| Style | Architecture | Resolve |
|---|---|---|
| APA | Author–year; alphabetical | Edition, disambiguation, locators |
| Harvard | Author–year | Institution/journal variant; no single universal version |
| Vancouver | Numeric; first occurrence | Parentheses/brackets/superscripts, NLM variant |
| IEEE | Numeric brackets | Current guide, venue exceptions |
| Chicago | Notes–bibliography OR author–date | Choose one system |
| MLA | Author–page | Edition and source-specific locator |

Follow an exact supplied style. If absent ask once, recommend, or adopt a delegated default. Flag a journal/style conflict rather than silently overriding either.

Map stable source IDs to citations at output. Numeric order follows first appearance; repeated sources keep one number. Renumber after moves. Respect numbering inherited from the surrounding manuscript; if unavailable say numbering is local. Check both directions between citations and reference list. Some bibliographies permit uncited background works; do not assume this.

Keep complete available metadata; deduplicate by identity. Cite books, chapters, datasets, software, preprints and web pages in their actual types. Label preprints and versions. Unknown fields are not absent fields. Never guess missing pages/volume. Review reference-manager output. Export BibTeX/RIS only if it can be generated faithfully; claim import testing only when performed.

## Attribution

Reconstruct a paraphrase in your own syntax and cite it. Translation still needs attribution. Exact wording needs quotation and a locator. APA uses inline quotation below 40 words and block quotation at 40 or more; other styles differ. Use direct quotations selectively. Never invent pages.

Prefer originals. If only a secondary source is read, disclose it; in APA list only the source actually read. Do not imply original access. Place citations where support scope is clear. Avoid a single ambiguous citation after several unrelated claims.

Use self-citations when relevant. Decline fabrication and irrelevant citation padding; offer real retrieval or placeholders instead. Do not use an AI answer as primary evidence for the scientific claim.
