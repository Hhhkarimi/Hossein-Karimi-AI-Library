# Draft audit

## Scientific alignment

Compare with abstract, methods, results and author answers. Resolve conflicting populations, endpoints, dates, constructs and designs. Distinguish association from causation. Preserve planned vs post hoc status; do not retrofit predictions after seeing results. Use planned language for protocols.

The gap must lead to the actual aim. Remove aims the study cannot address. Keep contribution proportional to the work. Explain practical importance separately from scholarly contribution when both are claimed.

## Prose

Check paragraph function, evidence, interpretation and connection. Replace source-by-source lists with supported synthesis. Preserve disagreements. Use terms consistently and define scope-sensitive constructs.

Write for the audience. Remove inflated openings, stock claims, ornamental transitions and unsupported superlatives. Keep necessary technical precision. Match certainty to evidence. Use active voice for clear agents and passive when the recipient matters. Follow rhetorical function and venue for tense and first person.

Keep English variety consistent. In Persian normalize ی/ک, half-spaces and terminology, while preserving Latin identifiers and metadata. Do not present a translated title as the source's published title. Preserve notation during editing.

Include enough method to orient, not a methods manual. Place detailed interpretation of current results in Discussion. Apply venue exceptions deliberately. Avoid duplicating an abstract or thesis chapter.

Keep explanations of your writing decisions in the author note. Phrases such as "this aim does not require a hypothesis" explain the drafting process and usually do not belong in manuscript prose. With thin evidence, write a shorter supported argument rather than padding with methodological truisms. Explain any resulting length shortfall.

## Audit dimensions

Mark pass, revise or unresolved; give concrete reasons for defects:

1. Problem and importance.
2. Synthesis of knowledge.
3. Gap or other rationale.
4. Contribution and nearest-work comparison.
5. Aims/design alignment.
6. Claim support and verification.
7. Citation/reference correspondence.
8. Structure and language.
9. Venue, length and format.

Apply repairs before delivery. A central unresolved item means provisional. Do not convert scores into acceptance probabilities. Count words by tool when available, otherwise label approximate; state inclusions. Source-checking is not an independent methods appraisal.
