# Article types and argument patterns

These routes guide judgment; they are not universal formats. Journal instructions and epistemology take precedence.

| Article | Emphasis | Typical ending |
|---|---|---|
| Quantitative | Problem, relevant evidence/theory | Aims and, where appropriate, hypotheses |
| Qualitative | Phenomenon, context, interpretive need | Open question and purpose |
| Mixed methods | Need for both strands and integration | Quantitative, qualitative and integration aims |
| Systematic review/meta-analysis | Uncertainty and need for another synthesis | Explicit question; PICO when suitable |
| Scoping review | Why mapping breadth/concepts fits | Population, concept, context when suitable |
| Narrative review | Scope and need for synthesis | Purpose and organizing lens |
| Method/tool | Limitation in existing practice | Method objective and evaluation scope |
| Theory/concept | Explanatory or conceptual problem | Thesis, propositions or framework |
| Clinical case | Distinctive/educational value | Focus of report |
| Humanities | Object and interpretive debate | Claim and approach |
| Replication | Consequential uncertainty in prior evidence | What is replicated and why |
| Protocol | Need for planned study | Planned aims, not outcomes |

Hypotheses are not mandatory in all quantitative work. Qualitative work can use theory deductively. Geographic difference needs a reason context might matter. Replication need not masquerade as unprecedented discovery. A case-study design is not a clinical case report.

## Structure

- Funnel: narrow from relevant context; avoid distant history.
- Problem-led: concrete problem and consequences.
- Gap-led: supported limitation of knowledge.
- Contradiction-led: inconsistent evidence; first examine method/context differences.
- Theory-led: framework to unresolved prediction or boundary.
- Method-led: task to limitations of current methods.
- Narrative opening: vignette to scholarly question; identify fictional/composite examples and protect anonymity.

CARS provides territory, niche and present work; moves can recur and overlap. A move is not a paragraph. Avoid duplicating a separate Related Work section.

## Plan

Use columns: paragraph, function, main claim, evidence IDs, next connection and approximate words. Adjust the budget after drafting. Avoid percentage rules. With no venue limit, propose a length suited to the article.

## Venue exceptions

ICMJE advises omitting current-study data and conclusions from the introduction. Other fields may preview findings. Inspect venue examples. Nature's summary paragraph is separate from main text and does not establish a universal rule.

Select reporting standards by design and check current versions when used. CONSORT, STROBE, PRISMA, JARS and CARE are not citation styles or proof of study quality. PICO does not fit every question.
