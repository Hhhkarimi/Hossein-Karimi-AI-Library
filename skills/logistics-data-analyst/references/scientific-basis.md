# Scientific Basis and Methodological Anchors

This skill is designed around well-established themes in logistics analytics, supply-chain analytics, forecasting, inventory control, and operations research.

## Core literature themes

### Supply-chain and logistics analytics
- Wang, G., Gunasekaran, A., Ngai, E. W. T., & Papadopoulos, T. (2016). Big data analytics in logistics and supply chain management: Certain investigations for research and applications. *International Journal of Production Economics*, 176, 98-110.
- Arunachalam, D., Kumar, N., & Kawalek, J. P. (2018). Understanding big data analytics capabilities in supply chain management: Unravelling the issues, challenges and implications for practice. *Transportation Research Part E*, 114, 416-436.
- Govindan, K., Cheng, T. C. E., Mishra, N., & Shukla, N. (2018). Big data analytics and application for logistics and supply chain management. *Transportation Research Part E*, 114, 343-349.

### Forecasting
- Hyndman, R. J., & Athanasopoulos, G. *Forecasting: Principles and Practice*. OTexts. Use time-aware validation, appropriate baselines, and scale-aware error metrics.
- Intermittent-demand literature motivates special treatment for sparse demand rather than assuming ordinary continuous demand behavior.

### Inventory
- Classical inventory theory links reorder policy and safety stock to demand uncertainty, replenishment lead time, service-level objectives, and cost trade-offs. Apply formulas only when assumptions are appropriate to the operating process.

### Transportation and routing
- Vehicle Routing Problem (VRP) research establishes routing as a constrained combinatorial optimization problem with many operational variants, including capacity and time-window constraints.
- Operations-research methods should distinguish proven optimum from heuristic or approximate solutions.

### Statistical discipline
- Use descriptive statistics to characterize distributions, inferential methods to quantify uncertainty, predictive validation for forecasts, and causal language only when supported by study design.

## Interpretation rules derived from the literature

1. Analytics capability is not only modeling; data integration, process knowledge, and organizational decision use are essential.
2. Logistics data is heterogeneous and operationally generated, so data-quality and semantic validation are prerequisites to model quality.
3. Forecasting quality must be evaluated out of sample and in a time-respecting way.
4. Optimization recommendations must encode real constraints and service requirements.
5. Operational decisions are multi-objective: cost, service, inventory, capacity, resilience, and risk can conflict.

## Use of citations

When the user requests a research-style deliverable:
- use current primary or peer-reviewed sources where available;
- separate sourced facts from your analysis;
- do not invent article details, DOI values, or numeric findings;
- verify recent claims before citing them.
